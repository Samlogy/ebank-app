title: Why we chose a gateway before a service mesh
date: '2025-04-03'
summary: A service mesh solves real problems — but not the ones a small platform has on day one. Here's the reasoning for starting with an API gateway and keeping the mesh on the roadmap.

"Should we run a service mesh?" is one of those questions where the honest answer
is *"eventually, probably — but not yet."* Here's how we reasoned about it.

## What a mesh actually buys you

mTLS everywhere, fine-grained traffic shaping, retries and circuit breaking at the
sidecar, and rich east-west observability. All genuinely useful. All with a cost:
a sidecar next to every pod, a control plane to operate, and a new failure mode to
learn.

## What we needed on day one

- One place to enforce auth and rate limits at the edge.
- Dynamic routing so scaling a service didn't mean editing config.
- Centralized configuration.

Every one of those is a **north-south** (edge) concern, and an API gateway plus a
discovery layer covers them without a sidecar in sight.

## The decision

We shipped **Spring Cloud Gateway + service discovery + a config server** — native
to the stack the team already knew — and wrote down the trigger for revisiting:

> When cross-service mTLS becomes a compliance requirement, or when we need
> per-route traffic shaping the gateway can't express, we adopt a mesh.

That's the part teams skip: not just *what* you chose, but *the condition under
which you'd choose differently*. A decision without a revisit trigger is just a
guess you've stopped questioning.

## The tradeoff

We accepted weaker east-west controls in exchange for far less operational
surface. For our traffic and team size, that was clearly the right trade — and
writing down the trigger means it stays right only as long as it actually is.
