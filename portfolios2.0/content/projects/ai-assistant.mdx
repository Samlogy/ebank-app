## Context

Support was answering the same account and product questions over and over. An
LLM was the obvious tool — but a raw chatbot that hallucinates balances or invents
policy is worse than no bot at all in a banking context. It needed to answer
*from our data* and be able to *take grounded actions*, not free-associate.

## My role

Solo — I designed and built the service end-to-end and, importantly, treated it as
**just another microservice**: same gateway, same pipeline, same observability. No
special-snowflake infrastructure.

## The decision

The key decision was **how to keep answers grounded**. Options:

- **Prompt-stuff everything** — dump docs into the context window. Simple, but it
  doesn't scale, gets expensive, and still drifts.
- **Fine-tune a model** — heavy, slow to iterate, and stale the moment a policy
  changes.
- **Retrieval-augmented generation over a vector store** — retrieve the few
  relevant chunks per question and ground the answer in them, with tool-calling for
  live data.

RAG won: answers cite current documents, updating knowledge means re-indexing (not
retraining), and **tool-calling** lets the model fetch live account data through
typed, permissioned functions instead of guessing.

- **Spring AI** for the LLM integration, embeddings and tool-calling wiring.
- **pgvector** as the vector store — one less piece of infrastructure since
  PostgreSQL was already in the stack.
- **Tool-calling** into existing services through typed functions, so the model
  reads real data instead of inventing it.
- Same **gateway, CI/CD and telemetry** as the rest of the platform.

## Challenges / tradeoffs

Early answers were confidently wrong when retrieval missed — the model would
**fill the gap with plausible fiction** rather than admit it didn't know.

- **Root cause:** no floor on retrieval relevance, and a prompt that rewarded
  answering over abstaining.
- **Fix:** added a similarity threshold (below it, the bot says it doesn't know and
  offers a handoff), tightened the system prompt to answer *only* from retrieved
  context, and logged retrieved chunks alongside each answer for auditability.
- **Tradeoff accepted:** the bot now declines more often. In a banking context, a
  clear "I don't know, here's a human" beats a confident wrong number every time.

## Outcome

- Grounded answers with an audit trail of what the model actually read.
- Knowledge updates are a re-index, not a retrain.
- Deployed and operated exactly like every other service — no bespoke AI
  infrastructure to babysit.
