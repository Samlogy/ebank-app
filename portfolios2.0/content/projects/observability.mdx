## Context

The platform was live before it was observable. When something was slow, the only
signal was a customer complaint, and debugging meant SSHing into containers to
tail logs. There was no on-call rotation — incidents landed on whoever noticed
first, which is a great way to burn out a team.

## My role

Solo on the platform side: I built the metrics/logging/tracing stack and then
designed the human side — the rotation, the escalation policy and the runbooks —
with the team's input.

## The decision

The temptation was to buy an all-in-one APM and move on. Instead I chose an
**OpenTelemetry-based, vendor-neutral pipeline** feeding Prometheus, a log store
and a tracing backend.

- **All-in-one SaaS APM** — fastest to a dashboard, but locks instrumentation to
  one vendor and gets expensive as traffic grows.
- **OpenTelemetry + open backends** — a bit more setup, but instrumentation is
  written once and the backend is swappable. For a platform meant to outlast any
  single tool, portability won.

The second decision was cultural: **alert on symptoms users feel** (latency,
error rate, saturation), not on every internal metric. Fewer, better alerts.

- **OpenTelemetry SDKs** in every service for consistent traces and metrics.
- **A collector tier** so services don't know or care where telemetry is stored.
- **SLO-based alerting** — pages fire on error-budget burn, not raw thresholds.
- **Runbooks** linked directly from each alert, plus a blameless postmortem
  template and a defined escalation path.

## Challenges / tradeoffs

The first alert set was **too noisy** — CPU and memory thresholds paged people at
3am for blips that self-resolved. Trust in the pager dropped fast, and ignored
alerts are worse than no alerts.

- **Root cause:** alerting on causes (resource metrics) instead of symptoms
  (user-facing SLIs).
- **Fix:** rewrote alerts around SLO burn rate — page only when the error budget
  is actually being spent fast enough to matter. Resource metrics moved to
  dashboards, not pages.
- **What changed after:** page volume dropped by roughly two-thirds, and the pages
  that remained were real. On-call stopped being a punishment.

## Outcome

- Mean time to *detection* went from "a customer told us" to minutes.
- Traces made cross-service latency debuggable without SSH.
- A fair, documented on-call rotation with actionable, low-noise alerts.
- Blameless postmortems turned incidents into durable fixes instead of blame.
